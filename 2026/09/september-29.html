The Poem Is Born from Her Fingers…

I was born…
on the twenty-ninth of September,
on that moody day
when the earth enters adolescence,
and the trees grow pregnant…

What happened in our house
on the night of the twenty-ninth of September,
stirring tears and sorrow?

Why did my mother look so pale?
She swallowed her screams,
tore at her bedsheets,
and cried out to the Virgin Mary
to help her through her labor…

No one answered me…
Yet I felt that a woman in our house
was living through a moment of creation…

I was born under the sign of Libra,
the sign of those madmen who decided
to steal fire from the heavens…

I emerged from my shell,
blood-stained like a fish…
and in my hand
was a piece of chalk searching for a wall…

Breaking things…
was my hobby and my profession,
and I had a craving to break free
from the cloak of my uncles…

The day they bought me
a pen… and a notebook,
I decided to belong
to the family of lightning,
not the family of stones…

I was born…
on the twenty-ninth of September.

And in my childhood,
as an old neighbor in our quarter used to say,
I was ever ready for love…
like a rooster…

My occupation
was to gather women into a bottle…
and gather flowers…

And when my father came home
at the end of the day,
he said to my mother, laughing:

“Rejoice, Nadia…
this one you gave birth to
is no child at all…
he is a hurricane…”

My mother’s milk was white ink,
and her breast taught me
the art of pottery…

I was born in Damascus,
among jasmine bowers,
wildflowers,
daffodils,
and dahlias…

And still, within my language,
there lingers a touch
of cinnamon, cumin, and spice…

My birthplace is Damascus of the Levant,
where the houses are a naked woman,
and across the whiteness of her breast
the rivers come of age…

It is a miracle
for a man to be born in a city
that casts upon his shoulders,
in summertime,
thousands of moons…

I never had a problem,
for everything here
I found already set to music:

the earth,
the sky,
the fields,
the birds,
the winds,
and the rain.

How can I say that I was born?

When I was still sitting in my mother’s womb
like a slaughtered chick…
waiting for them to take my mother
to the delivery table…

My birth…
was unprecedented.

The doctor did not pull out
my head first…
instead,
I handed him my fingers…

The letters of the heart have turned gray,
my lady,
and the papers and pens have turned gray too.

Yet for a thousand…
a thousand years,
I remain
in the delivery room…

waiting for my other birth
at your hands…

waiting for you, my lady,
to open the cages…

so that the doves
may fly free…
